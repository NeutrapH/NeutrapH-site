Reverse osmosis, explained

Pressure in. Two streams out.

Reverse osmosis (RO) uses pressure to push water through a semi-permeable membrane. The membrane allows water through while reducing many dissolved salts and other substances.

01 / PRESSURE

Why “reverse”?

In natural osmosis, water moves across a membrane toward the more concentrated solution. RO applies pressure to drive water in the opposite direction.

02 / SEPARATION

Where do the salts go?

Many dissolved salts remain in the concentrate stream. This water leaves separately; it is not the finished drinking-water stream.

03 / VERIFICATION

Is RO alone enough?

It is one part of a treatment system. Pre-treatment, maintenance, hygienic storage and water-quality testing all matter.

Good questions

Clear water deserves clear answers.

Does clear-looking water mean safe water?

No. Appearance cannot tell you everything about dissolved chemicals or microorganisms. Appropriate testing is needed to assess water quality.

Does RO remove minerals too?

RO reduces many dissolved mineral salts. The finished-water composition depends on the source, membrane and system operation. Any conditioning must be considered in the final design.

Does the system use every drop?

No. RO produces both permeate and concentrate. Their proportions depend on the design and operating conditions; NeutrapH has not confirmed a recovery rate.

What has NeutrapH verified so far?

The treatment journey shown here is proposed. Water-test evidence is pending and no finished-water test report is available on this website yet. NeutrapH has not started trading.
